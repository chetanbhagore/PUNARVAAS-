### 1. PROBLEM ALIGNMENT & SOLUTION RELEVANCE — 22 / 25

**What they did well:**
- Directly addresses all 4 hazard types mentioned in the PS
- The 4-layer composite formula (Susceptibility + Trigger + Vulnerability + History) maps
  perfectly to what the PS calls "hazard intensity + population vulnerability + disaster history"
- Relocation urgency tiers (IMMEDIATE / SHORT_TERM / MEDIUM_TERM) directly match PS language
- Proactive planning angle is genuinely addressed — not reactive

**Where marks are lost (-3):**
- The PS says **"real-time"** and **"dynamically identify and update"** Red Zones.
  Your system uses **pre-cached synthetic data with scenario buttons**.
  A judge who reads carefully will push on this: *"Show me real-time update."*
  You cannot. This is the single biggest gap between PS expectation and your delivery.
- The term **"GIS-enabled"** in the PS implies spatial querying, buffer zones, vector layers.
  Your Leaflet map shows markers — it does NOT do GIS operations (proximity analysis,
  flood extent polygons, watershed delineation). A GIS-literate judge will notice.

### 2. TECHNICAL DEPTH & INNOVATION — 20 / 25

**What they did well:**
- Hazard-specific trigger formulas anchored to GSI (150mm), CWC (92.40m DL / 94.35m HFL),
  and IMD categorical mapping — this is genuinely impressive technical grounding
- Dual-model validation (rule-based vs ML) with "Review Needed" flag at >0.15 divergence —
  this is a real data science pattern, not a gimmick
- Capacity-constrained greedy allocation with split allocation and deficit tracking —
  algorithmically correct and well-implemented
- Evacuation state machine (UNCONTACTED → CONTACTED → PICKED_UP → CHECKED_IN → BLOCKED) —
  demonstrates real operational thinking

**Where marks are lost (-5):**
- **LogisticRegression trained on its own synthetic data** — this is the most fragile point
  technically. The ML model is trained on data that was GENERATED by the rule engine.
  So ML ≈ rule engine. They will always agree. The "Review Needed" flag is cosmetic.
  A data scientist judge will ask: *"What is the F1 score? What is the train/test split?"*
  You have no answer because there is no real ground truth.
- **No real GIS layer** — flood extent rasters, DEM-based slope analysis, satellite imagery —
  none of these exist. The PS explicitly says GIS-enabled.
- **Only 6 ML features** — real risk models use 20-40 features. Judges may call this simplistic.

## CRITICAL QUESTIONS A JUDGE WILL ASK YOU

Prepare verbal answers for ALL of these. If you stumble, marks will drop.

### Q1 (Most Dangerous):
> *"Your system claims real-time Red Zone updates. Show me what happens when
> I enter a new rainfall reading of 200mm for Kandhamal right now."*

**Your honest answer must be:**
*"In this prototype, we simulate it via the Scenario Mode button — the scenario engine

### Q2 (Technical Trap):
> *"Your ML model is trained on synthetic data generated by your own rule engine.
> Isn't that just the rule engine predicting itself?"*

**Your honest answer must be:**
*"You are correct that in this prototype they are correlated. The ML component's

dual-model validation, and Review Needed flagging. In production, it would be retrained

### Q3 (GIS Challenge):

**Your honest answer must be:**
*"The spatial layer is implemented via Leaflet with coordinate-precise habitation

flood extent WMS layers from NRSC Bhuvan, DEM-based slope polygons) is architecturally
planned for V3.0. The scoring and relocation engine are spatially aware through
lat/lon and district-level administrative geometry."*

---

**Your honest answer must be:**
*"All per-village readings are synthetic. The thresholds are real:

cyclone categories directly from IMD classification standards.
Every historical event (Fani, Phailin, 1999 Super Cyclone) is a real documented event.

In production, we connect to IMD API, CWC telemetry, and NDMA's habitation registry."*
